Digital twins of Saudi youth

Test it on the twins first.

One programme has to build critical thinking in very different kids: an 8-year-old in Tabuk, a gifted 16-year-old in Riyadh, a 13-year-old in a wheelchair in Jeddah. Today you find out who it fails after the pilot. Digital twins show you before: how each kid thinks, where the session fails them, and what to change.

01

Meet the twins

Five kids from the brief. Each twin carries the kid’s age, life, barriers and way of learning, plus a starting level in the four parts of critical thinking.

Starting levels: Starting · Growing · Solid · Strong. The levels and the twins’ words are sample content.

02

Run one session

The Community Council from the brief: five kids decide whether an empty plot becomes a park, a sports facility or a mall. The pack hides one claim with no source. Play it and read each twin’s reply. Every reply is tagged for the part of critical thinking it practises or misses.

03

See who it fails, and why

The session works for one kid. For the other four, the twins show the reason. Two are design problems inside the session. Two are barriers that stop the kid from taking part at all.

Results · as designed · sample

04

Change it. Run it again.

The twins point to one change per kid. Turn the changes on, then run the session again. Try it with some changes off, too: one kid’s change can decide if the others get heard.

05

Then test every format

Run the same test for all five formats in the brief. The map shows how many of the four parts each kid practises in each format. It tells you what to pilot first, before you spend on the pilot. Click any cell for the reason.
